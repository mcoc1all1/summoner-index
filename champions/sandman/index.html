TACTIC 1

Bank Resistance Ups before Unstable

Sandman's Unstable state spends Resistance Up resources on eligible attacks. Check how many Resistance Ups remain and whether the defender offers a safe knockdown opening before forcing a Heavy near the wall; the charge count matters more than a memorized combo.

TACTIC 2

Choose a knockdown loop for the fight

While Unstable, his knockdowns and follow-up attacks can generate physical burst damage. SP3 can support a different damage plan from SP1 or SP2, so prioritize the route suited to the opponent's remaining health and special access.

TACTIC 3

Keep the utility route separate from peak damage

When a defender requires healing or buff-related utility, a shorter special route may be more useful than trying to reach the largest burst. Kabam's rebalance specifically addressed the damage gap between SP3 and SP1/SP2 loops, making older numbers unreliable.

TACTIC 4

Check armor and damage-form restrictions

Sand Armor can mitigate certain large hits while active, but a spent layer needs recovery and is not full invulnerability. Confirm the actual armor state and effect-specific immunities before using Sandman against passive damage or a dangerous node.

Attacker starting point
Block Proficiency

Block Proficiency can reduce chip damage while building Resistance Ups and preparing a rooted Heavy Unstable cycle; it does not shorten the path to maximum stacks or strengthen the root. Compare Special Damage at matched stacks.

Physical Resistance reduces eligible physical damage only; it cannot compensate for a critical-hit trigger that removes the Resistance needed to remain Unstable. Compare Block Proficiency for block-heavy attacker matchups.
