Champion-specific fight decisions

Champion-specific setup, burst, matchup and failure-condition guidance independently written from the research catalog. These decisions are not site-tested clears; confirm the current live kit and node before relying on them. Checked 2026-11-05; confirm your current champion version, nodes and matchup.

TACTIC 1

Read Cleanse and Armor cooldown together

Baron Zemo has Cleanse resources linked to an armor cooldown. Verify which Cleanse effects are currently available before deliberately taking a debuff; passive damage and different node interactions may not produce the same result.

TACTIC 2

Build Fury from legitimate Purify triggers

Certain opponent Purify or immunity interactions can grant Zemo Fury, but post-release balance adjustments changed how abusive node interactions behave. Inspect the live Fury stack rather than counting on endless free stacks from an older example.

TACTIC 3

Use Root and Bleed from a safe punish

Root pressure can create strong offensive windows but also demands close spacing. Bait the opponent's Special Attack, verify Bleed can apply, and use a real recovery punish instead of forcing the next attack while the defender remains active.

TACTIC 4

Do not mistake Cleanse for universal immunity

Cleanse is not protection against every passive or instant damage effect. Kabam changed both Fury and Cleanse-associated interactions after his launch, so check the current kit and the opponent's node before using him as a guaranteed counter.

Attacker starting point
Critical Damage Rating

Editorial comparison: Baron Zemo builds Root windows and indefinite Bleeds. Critical Damage Rating may benefit eligible critical direct hits, but it should not be assumed to increase the indefinite Bleed damage created during Root. Compare equal Root duration and Bleed stacks, and test a setup-survival alternative when opponents resist Bleed.

Defender starting point
Block Penetration

Block Penetration is a conditional defender-pressure hypothesis for attacks that connect into Block; it does not prevent Root escape, cleanse, or make the indefinite Bleed stack unavoidable. A mitigation focus is a better comparison when the attacker rarely blocks.
